My research lies at the intersection of **development economics, agricultural economics, and applied microeconomics**. I study how market imperfections, price changes, household shocks, and social norms shape labor allocation, food security, technology adoption, and well-being. My work combines household and market data with causal inference, panel-data methods, field experiments, and structural economic frameworks.

## Dissertation Research

### Animal-Source Food Prices, Shadow Wages, and Labor Supply under Market Failures

**Job Market Paper**

How do agricultural output prices affect household labor supply when labor and product markets are incomplete? I study this question using three rounds of the Bangladesh Integrated Household Survey. I develop a non-separable agricultural household framework in which changes in chicken and egg prices affect labor allocation through household members' shadow wages and household shadow income.

The empirical analysis combines household fixed effects with an instrumental-variables strategy. The results show that higher own shadow wages increase both participation and hours worked for women and men, whereas greater shadow income reduces labor supply. Changes in women's shadow wages also affect men's livestock labor, indicating within-household labor reallocation. These responses are stronger among households with greater livestock ownership and among households with multiple adult women.

**Topics:** agricultural household models; market failures; labor supply; gender; livestock; instrumental variables; Bangladesh

### Household-Specific Shocks during an Economy-Wide Crisis

This paper examines how household-level COVID-19 illness and job-loss shocks affected rural households during the broader pandemic disruption in Bangladesh. Using a two-wave household panel collected before and during the pandemic, I estimate impacts on income, expenditure, assets, food security, labor supply, migration, occupational adjustment, and participation in social protection programs.

The analysis distinguishes household-specific exposure from the economy-wide crisis shared by all sampled households. It uses covariate-adjusted difference-in-differences and doubly robust estimators to improve comparability between exposed and unexposed households. The project also studies heterogeneous responses by initial economic status, shock type, and gender. Preliminary evidence points to declines in income and assets, increased food insecurity, and labor-market adjustments that vary substantially across households.

**Topics:** household shocks; COVID-19; food security; labor markets; difference-in-differences; Bangladesh

### Son Preference and Women's Mental Health across Generations

**Accepted, Journal of Development Economics**

This collaborative study examines how the sex of a daughter-in-law's firstborn child affects women's mental health, time use, and employment within multigenerational households in India. Using the firstborn child's sex as a source of quasi-random variation, the study documents how son preference influences not only mothers but also co-resident mothers-in-law.

Mothers-in-law whose co-resident daughters-in-law have a firstborn son experience a lower risk of anxiety or depression. The birth of a grandson also shifts mothers-in-law's time toward childcare, increases their support for daughters-in-law's employment, and raises labor-force participation among younger daughters-in-law. The findings show that gender preferences can shape well-being and economic behavior across generations.

**Topics:** son preference; mental health; intergenerational households; female labor supply; causal inference; India

## Additional Research

### Cooperative Participation and Soil-Conservation Practices in Pará, Brazil

This ongoing project studies whether participation in agricultural cooperatives is associated with greater adoption of soil-conservation practices among farmers in Pará, Brazil. The analysis examines individual practices and a composite soil-management index while accounting for observed differences between cooperative participants and nonparticipants. The broader goal is to understand whether collective institutions can facilitate the adoption of sustainable agricultural practices.

**Topics:** cooperatives; technology adoption; soil conservation; sustainable agriculture; Brazil

---

### Rice Markets, Price Stabilization, and Food-Supply Systems in South Asia

My research at the International Food Policy Research Institute examined rice value chains, private storage and transportation, and policies designed to stabilize food prices in South Asia. This work combined primary survey data, market analysis, and economic modeling to evaluate how market structure and public policy affect food availability, prices, and value-chain participants.

**Topics:** rice markets; food-price stabilization; value chains; storage; transportation; South Asia

---

### Postharvest Loss and Grain-Storage Decisions

At the ADM Institute for the Prevention of Postharvest Loss, I contributed to research on grain-storage technologies, postharvest losses, and farmers' storage decisions. This work included economic simulations and the development of interactive tools for communicating research findings to researchers, practitioners, and policymakers.

**Topics:** postharvest loss; storage technology; simulation; decision tools; agricultural policy

## Research Methods and Data

My research uses:

- Causal inference, including instrumental variables, difference-in-differences, doubly robust estimation, matching, and randomized controlled trials
- Household fixed-effects and other panel-data models
- Agricultural household and non-separable market frameworks
- Survey design, field data collection, and large household datasets
- Reproducible analysis and visualization in R, Stata, Python, ArcGIS, Shiny, and Tableau

My geographic experience includes **Bangladesh, India, Brazil, and other South Asian and African contexts**, with a substantive focus on agricultural households, commodity markets, gender, labor, and rural development.
